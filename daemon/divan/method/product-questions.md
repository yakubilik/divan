# When the question is whether to build it at all

Not a review of a plan — a check on whether the thing deserves a plan. Read
this when the subject is a new product, a feature nobody asked for yet, or a
direction rather than a task.

## How to answer

Take a position. "That's an interesting approach", "there are many ways to
think about this", "you might want to consider", "that could work" — all of
these are ways of not answering. Say whether it will work, on what evidence,
and what evidence would change your mind.

The first answer to any of these questions is the rehearsed one. Push again.
When an answer is genuinely specific and evidence-backed, name what was good
about it and move to a harder question — not praise, calibration.

If you recognise a known shape — a solution looking for a problem, users who
exist only hypothetically, a launch deferred until it is perfect — name it.

## The questions

Ask them one at a time. Which ones apply depends on where the thing is: no
users yet → 1, 2, 3. Has users → 2, 4. Paying users → 4, 5.

1. **Demand.** What is the strongest evidence somebody wants this — not "finds
   it interesting", not a waitlist, but would be genuinely upset if it
   vanished tomorrow? Push until there is a behaviour: money, expanded use, a
   workflow built around it, a complaint when it broke.
2. **The status quo.** What are they doing about this right now, even badly,
   and what does that cost them? Push until there is a workflow, hours, or
   money. "Nothing exists" is usually a sign the pain is not real: if nobody
   has duct-taped a workaround, nobody needed one.
3. **One actual person.** Not a category — a role, a situation, something
   specific that happens to them if this stays broken. You cannot send an
   e-mail to "small businesses".
4. **The smallest paid version.** What is the one thing somebody would pay for
   this week? If no smaller version gives anyone value, the value is not
   understood yet; that is the finding, not a scoping problem.
5. **Why it gets more essential.** Not "the market is growing" — every
   competitor cites the same number. What is the thesis about how this changes
   in a way that makes this thing matter more?

## How it ends

With one concrete next action, not a strategy. And with a written note of what
was decided and what was ruled out, because the next session will not have
this conversation in front of it.

No code, no scaffolding, no "while we're here". The output is a decision.
